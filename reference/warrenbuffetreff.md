# Warren Buffett Lens — Checklist

Apply this as a qualitative + quantitative filter, drawing on Buffett's well-documented public writings (shareholder letters, interviews) — do not invent quotes or attribute fabricated statements to him.

## 1. Understandable business
- Can the business be explained in a few plain sentences? Avoid businesses whose earnings depend on things that are hard to forecast (e.g., commodity price swings, fast-moving tech cycles) unless the moat is unusually clear.

## 2. Durable competitive advantage ("moat")
Look for evidence of, not just claims of:
- Pricing power: can the company raise prices without losing volume? (check gross margin stability/expansion through cost-inflation periods)
- Brand strength, network effects, switching costs, cost advantages, or regulatory/license barriers
- Market share trend — gaining or losing?

## 3. Consistent, predictable earnings
- 10-year (or longest available) net income and EPS history — look for steady upward trend without wild swings
- Avoid companies with a history of loss years unless there's a clear, credible turnaround story

## 4. High and consistent returns on capital
- ROE consistently above ~15% over many years, without excessive leverage inflating it (cross-check against ROIC and Debt/Equity together)
- ROIC durably above cost of capital

## 5. Conservative financing
- Low debt relative to earnings (Net debt/EBITDA, interest coverage)
- Ability to fund growth without constantly issuing new shares or debt

## 6. Rational, shareholder-oriented management (capital allocation)
- Track record of capital allocation: reinvestment in the business vs dividends vs buybacks — done at sensible valuations, not just to offset stock-comp dilution
- Insider ownership and insider buying as an alignment signal
- Candid, plain-English communication in shareholder letters/filings (a soft signal, but one Buffett explicitly values)

## 7. Margin of safety
- Estimate rough intrinsic value (owner earnings based, or a simple DCF) and compare to current price
- Buffett's heuristic: buy a wonderful business at a fair price, not a fair business at a wonderful price — a great checklist score at a very expensive valuation is still a "wait" not a "buy"
- If the user wants an actual intrinsic-value number, offer to build a simple owner-earnings DCF with their own growth/discount-rate assumptions rather than presenting a single "true value" as fact.

## Verdict framing
Summarize as: moat strength (weak/moderate/strong), earnings consistency (low/medium/high), balance sheet conservatism (weak/moderate/strong), capital allocation quality, and a valuation comment (cheap/fair/expensive relative to the quality found) — not a buy/sell instruction.
